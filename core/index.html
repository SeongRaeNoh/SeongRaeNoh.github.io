01 · The observation

Regrouping the options changes the probabilities

Vision decision models answer a multiple-choice question about an image with one probability per option, and software uses these probabilities directly. The options are mutually exclusive, so a merged option “A or B” should receive the probability of A plus that of B. It receives less.

−11.5pts

A merged option falls short of its members

Mean additivity error of the d3-lite base model over the 3,427 merged options of the evaluation set: the merged option’s probability minus the sum of its members’ probabilities.

It appears at every model size

Mean additivity error of the base model from 0.85B to 26B parameters; Core brings it close to zero at each size.

32%

Fine-tuning on labels does not remove it

After supervised fine-tuning (SFT), 32% of merged options still receive at least 0.05 less than their most probable member (base model: 31%).

18.6%
The grouping changes decisions. At a threshold of 0.5, the decision taken from a merged option differs from the one implied by the original question for 18.6% of merged options with the base model, 8.4% with LLM-as-Jev† and 7.4% with Core (d3-lite).

03 · Method

One target for every way of asking

Core (coherence under regrouping) asks each training item with its options regrouped and trains the answers to all these questions toward one target distribution over the original options, summed into each question’s groups. Numbers below are illustrative.
