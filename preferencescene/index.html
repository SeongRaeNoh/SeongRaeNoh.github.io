01 · The task

Preferences that the instruction does not state

Many preferences surface only when a person sees a concrete room. In our task, a system shows a 3D room for three turns; the user sees only rendered images of it and replies with brief free-form feedback and a satisfaction score. The system must then deliver a final room that shows the user's hidden preferences.

The input states part of the preferences, or none

Each user holds hidden values for ten design attributes of the room. The persona and instruction state four to six of them in the Explicit form and none in the Implicit form, where they only hint at the person's taste.

47.8%

Published systems cannot change most parts of the room

Given the hidden preferences directly as text, the five published text-to-scene baselines show at most 47.8% of them in their scenes: they at most pick wall and floor materials from a fixed library. PreferenceScene shows 59.4% in the same condition.

How a scene is scored: pAMR

The perceived attribute match rate is the share of the ten hidden values that a vision-language model (GLM-4.6V-Flash) recovers from two renders of the final scene, a cutaway view from above and an eye-level view.

pAMR = (1/10) · Σa 1[ answer of the evaluator for attribute a = hidden value of a ]

For each attribute, the evaluator answers one multiple-choice question whose options are all values of that attribute, once with the options in the given order and once reversed; pAMR averages the two answers. It is computed on images alone, so it applies to any system that outputs a scene.

03 · Method

An explicit plan of the whole room, revised after every turn

PreferenceScene keeps its estimate of the user's preferences as a plan: one value, a short text such as “pale oak planks”, for each of the ten design attributes of the room. Every change of the plan shows in the room, because scene instantiation builds each value as its own part of the room. The example below is the interaction of the paper's method figure (Implicit form, dining room); every value and text in it is a stored output of that run.

What each LLM call sees

Candidates (once): room type, instruction and persona; the LLM lists the visible design aspects of the room with twelve candidate values each, six that it considers most likely for this person and six other common choices. Jev, a decision model that returns a probability for every option of a multiple-choice question, assigns each aspect to its most probable attribute if that probability is at least 0.5.

Plan update (after every turn): the input, the candidate sets and, for every earlier turn, the plan, a description of what the scene contained, the feedback and the score. A value is either a candidate or a new value written in the same form.

Furnishing (once): the final plan; the reply adds furniture, objects on furniture tops and pieces for the walls in the free space and lists the object types that each value rules out.

An interaction makes six LLM calls and about 25 requests to Jev. No component reads the hidden values or the value sets of the benchmark.

04 · Results

Higher preference match than every baseline

Test set: 120 instances per form, four seeds each, three turns. Baselines: five published text-to-scene methods (Holodeck, LayoutGPT, I-Design, LayoutVLM, R3L), each run in the same interaction with the history as input, and Holodeck-Edit, which edits its previous scene in every turn. All systems use the same LLM (Gemma-4-31B), room shells, 6,793 Objaverse assets, renderer and cameras.

Bars: share of answers that chose PreferenceScene, with 95% Wilson intervals. Every rate differs from 50% (two-sided binomial test, Benjamini-Hochberg correction over 12 tests, p < 0.0001).

No iteration scores the scene of the initial plan, so its distance to the full method is the whole effect of the feedback. Without older turns, the plan update reads only the latest turn. Without new values, a value outside the candidates is rejected; only about one value in six of the full method's final plans is new (16.2% and 16.6%).

Parts of the room that the feedback never names

Other evaluators. With a second evaluator from another model family (Ministral-3-14B), PreferenceScene scores 52.5 (Explicit) and 46.5 (Implicit), against at most 44.9 and 43.0 for the baselines. With an evaluator variant that may answer “not visible”, it scores 47.8 and 40.9, against at most 35.8 and 33.4.
